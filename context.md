## The subject

**Gustavo Corção Braga** (1896–1978): Brazilian engineer-professor who, after
his first wife's death (1936) and a conversion through Chesterton and Maritain
(1939), became one of the country's best-known Catholic writers — the
conversion narrative *A Descoberta do Outro* (1944), the novel *Lições de
Abismo* (1950), a newspaper column that ran for over three decades across the
major Rio papers. He rose inside the **Centro Dom Vital** under Alceu Amoroso
Lima, led it during Alceu's absence (1951–53), and left it in **1963** with
about 200 associates in the dispute over the Church of dialogue and the
Council. Out of that exit he founded **Permanência** (1968) — association,
revista and editora at once, still active today under continuators who
describe themselves in traditionalist terms. He supported the 1964 coup in
print and, in the critical scholarship's reading, became an "apostle of the
linha-dura"; he clashed publicly with the Rio hierarchy under Dom Eugênio
Sales (1972–73) and broke with the Brazilian Benedictine Congregation (1974),
having been an oblate since his conversion years. He went progressively blind
in the 1970s and died 6 July 1978.

Why this project exists in the family: the `olavo` project keeps meeting
Corção as the figure Olavo de Carvalho called himself a reader and admirer of,
and as the "Olavo of the 1960s" of a press comparison he rejected. This repo
gives Corção one documented, dated place to point at.

## The posture: attributed, never adjudicated

This is an intellectual-biography project in `olavo`'s pattern. Life and works
are documented; characterizations are **attributed with author and date, never
adjudicated**. The load-bearing cases:

1. **The military-regime thread runs both directions.** Jalles de Paula
   (critical scholarship) documents the 1964 columns hailing the coup and
   reads Corção as "um dos artífices… da legitimação das bases
   antidemocráticas" of the regime. The sympathetic sources (Centro Dom Bosco,
   Mosteiro da Santa Cruz, admirers' chronologies) foreground the Catholic
   apologist and omit the regime thread — the Centro Dom Bosco profile never
   mentions the regime at all. The dataset records both framings with their
   authors; the site's voice takes neither.
2. **There is NO documented Corção–TFP rupture.** This premise is corrected
   and must not regress. What the sources establish: adversarial relations in
   the 1950s, then **convergence circa 1969** — Jalles: "unindo-se a antigos
   adversários" in the Dominicans campaign. Three framings of the relation
   exist and are all recorded, none asserted: (i) the DHBB's claim that
   Permanência was among "publicações dadas à… TFP"; (ii) Permanência's own
   TFP-silent independent-foundation narrative, which contradicts the DHBB;
   (iii) Jalles's former-adversaries-later-allies reading. The documented
   ruptures are with **Alceu / the Centro Dom Vital (1963)**, **the hierarchy
   (Dom Eugênio Sales, 1972–73)**, and **the Benedictine Congregation
   (1974)** — plus, per Jalles, with **Maritain** in the Vatican II years.
3. **Permanência's founding is layered, not contradictory.** Fleichman
   distinguishes a political founding (the August 1968 launch conference,
   press-dated), a juridical founding (his own act), and a religious founding
   (Cardinal Câmara's mass, 29 September 1968 — the date Permanência keeps).
   Both dates are in the chronology with their sources. The revista's first
   issue is not dated anywhere verified; earliest press anchor O Cruzeiro
   12/10/1968.
4. **Book years are discordant across sources** (*As Fronteiras da Técnica*
   spans 1951–1954 across four sources; *O Desconcerto do Mundo* 1961/1964/
   1965; the *Lições de Abismo* prize 1954/1955, UNESCO or IBCC). Flagged per
   work in the "Principal works" fact — never silently chosen.

## The reception layer (COF corpus)

A small thread only: Corção appears in Olavo de Carvalho's lecture corpus (the
community COF transcriptions) as recommended reading and in a 2012 discussion
of a Permanência response to Olavo. Corpus material is **paraphrase with file
id, date and review status; verbatim quotation REQUIRES AUDIO** verification.
The contested "morreu doido" family claim is double-attributed (Olavo, in a
2015 lecture, reports that the family is said to have claimed…) and asserted
by no one. See `KEYWORDS.md` for the corpus search traps ("Corsão", the
known-zero routes) before sweeping.

## Disambiguations that matter

- **Permanência** is three things — association, revista, editora — and the
  DHBB's TFP-linkage of it is that dictionary's claim, contradicted by
  Permanência's own account.
- **Corção admired-by vs. compared-to Olavo de Carvalho** are distinct claims
  with different evidence (corpus loci vs. a 2019 press comparison he
  rejected); the written record awaits Wayback/print capture since
  olavodecarvalho.org was hijacked.
- **Present-day Permanência ties** (Mosteiro da Santa Cruz, FSSPX milieu)
  rest on the continuators' own pages — labeled self-description.
